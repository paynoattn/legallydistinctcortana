# Focus reader: read aloud with the spoken word lit

Preconditions: Windows 11 with speakers or headphones, ideally two monitors. A Wyoming Piper server is reachable (see [read-aloud](read-aloud.md)), and the voice server in Settings points at it. The app runs with `make dev` (or an installed build). Nothing else uses Ctrl+Alt+R. Windows animation effects are on (Settings > Accessibility > Visual effects). In Edge, open a long article with several paragraphs.

1. Select three paragraphs of the article and press Ctrl+Alt+R. → Expect: the reader opens centred on the monitor under the mouse pointer, over Edge, and starts reading within half a second, in the last mood used (Neutral the first time) and the last voice. The title row says "Read aloud" with a Close button and no "Reading from" line. The text is split into the same three paragraphs. The mood button and the voice button show the mood and voice, the status says "Sentence 1 of N.", the keyboard hint is on one line under the controls, and the Pause button has focus.
2. Watch and listen for a minute. → Expect: one word is lit at a time (dark text on a yellow light). It stays on the spoken word or one either side, and is back on the spoken word at the start of each sentence. The sentence being read is bright, sentences ahead are dimmer, sentences already read are a muted blue-grey. On the same line the light glides between words; on a new line it jumps. The lit line stays about a third of the way down, and the thin yellow line above the controls grows as sentences finish.
3. Press Space. → Expect: the voice stops, the light stops, the status says "Paused. Sentence K of N." Press Space again. → Expect: reading continues from the start of the same sentence.
4. Press →. → Expect: reading jumps to the next sentence. Within a second, press ←. → Expect: reading goes back to the sentence before. Wait three seconds and press ←. → Expect: the current sentence starts again from its beginning. Click the Next sentence and Previous sentence buttons. → Expect: the same.
5. Click a word two paragraphs down. → Expect: reading continues from the start of that word's sentence, not from the word.
6. Press 2. → Expect: the current sentence starts again in a calmer, slower voice, and the mood button says Calm. Click the mood button and choose Whisper. → Expect: the current sentence starts again whispered, and the button says Whisper.
7. Click the voice button and pick a different voice. → Expect: the current sentence starts again in that voice, and the voice button shows its name.
8. Press Space to pause, then press 3. → Expect: reading starts from the paused sentence in the Cheerful mood. Pause again and click a word in a later sentence. → Expect: reading starts from that sentence.
9. Press Ctrl and + once. → Expect: the text grows a step. Press it ten more times, then Ctrl and − ten times. → Expect: the text stops growing at a limit and stops shrinking at a smaller limit, and the lit word stays lit throughout. Press Ctrl and + on the numpad. → Expect: the text grows a step.
10. Scroll up with the mouse wheel. → Expect: the text stops following the voice and a "Follow the voice" button appears. Click it. → Expect: the lit line scrolls back into view, about a third of the way down, and keeps following. Drag the scrollbar. → Expect: "Follow the voice" appears again.
11. Click into Edge. → Expect: the reader stays open and keeps reading.
12. Select a different paragraph in Edge and press Ctrl+Alt+R. → Expect: the reader shows the new paragraph and starts reading it from its first sentence.
13. Click into the reader and press Esc. → Expect: the voice stops, the reader closes, and focus is back in Edge (the article responds to the arrow keys).
14. Select a paragraph and press Ctrl+Alt+R. → Expect: the text is the size you left it at in step 9, and the mood is Cheerful (from step 8). Click the Close button in the title row. → Expect: the same as Esc: the voice stops, the reader closes, and focus is back in Edge.
15. Select a short paragraph, press Ctrl+Alt+R, and let it read to the end. → Expect: the status says "Finished reading.", no word is lit, and the progress line turns dim. Press Space. → Expect: it reads again from the first sentence.
16. Turn off Windows animation effects (Settings > Accessibility > Visual effects), then read a paragraph aloud. → Expect: the light jumps from word to word without gliding, and following scrolls instantly. Turn animation effects back on.
17. Move the mouse to the second monitor, select text there, and press Ctrl+Alt+R. → Expect: the reader opens centred on that monitor.
18. Click an empty area of the desktop so nothing is selected and press Ctrl+Alt+R. → Expect: the reader shows "Select some text first, then press the read-aloud shortcut." with a focused Close button, and nothing is read. Press Close. → Expect: the reader closes.
19. Stop the Piper server, select text, and press Ctrl+Alt+R. → Expect: the status shows "can't reach the voice server at <host>:<port>: …", no word is lit, and nothing is read. Press Esc. → Expect: the reader closes.
20. With Narrator on (Ctrl+Win+Enter), open the reader on a paragraph. → Expect: Narrator announces the status line as it changes but does not announce each lit word, and reading the text with Narrator works like a normal document.

Cleanup: restart the Piper server, turn Narrator off, and make sure Windows animation effects are on. In a reader, press 1 to set the remembered mood back to Neutral, set the voice back to the one you started with, and bring the text size back with Ctrl and + or − (if it no longer lands on the starting size, clearing the read-aloud window's local storage resets it, along with the mood and voice).
